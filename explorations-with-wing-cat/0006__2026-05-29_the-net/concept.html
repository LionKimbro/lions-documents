Concept

Memory Intake System

Catchbasket exists because the Internet produces more interesting material than a person can classify in the moment. The system gives those sparks a temporary body.

Its promise is deliberately modest: preserve the artifact, preserve the moment, and allow quick tagging without requiring a final decision.

What it is

A holding pen

It keeps useful, beautiful, strange, or maybe-useful things from vanishing before they have a proper home.

A low-friction ritual

The user should never have to write a careful note just to save a thing. Rough tags are enough.

A fossil of attention

The record says: this artifact crossed Lion's attention at this time, with these immediate associations.

Boundary

What this system should resist becoming

It should not require writing. Optional descriptions are useful, but mandatory descriptions make capture brittle.

It should not demand taxonomy at the door. The user is often saving an impression, not filing a document.

It should not become search-first too early. Search matters once the basket contains hundreds of records. Before that, reliable intake matters more.

New layer

Attention happens in clusters

A saved image is often not self-explanatory. Its meaning may live in the page around it, the article that led to it, the search trail that produced it, or the other things captured nearby.

That makes provenance part of memory, not metadata trivia. Catchbasket should preserve the object and the browsing moment that framed it.
