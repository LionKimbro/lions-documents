Glossary

Terms used in the report

Short definitions for the technical language behind the conversational AI architecture.

VAD

Voice Activity Detection. A small audio system that decides whether someone is currently speaking or whether the microphone is hearing silence, background noise, or non-speech sound.

LLM

Large Language Model. The text reasoning model that can answer questions, summarize, classify, plan, or produce structured data from prompts and context.

Speech Recognition

Technology that turns spoken audio into written words. It answers “what was said?” rather than “who said it?”

Diarization

Speaker separation over time. It labels audio segments as Speaker A, Speaker B, and so on, without necessarily knowing those speakers’ real identities.

Voice Recognition

Technology that tries to identify a known speaker from their voice. In this report, it should be consent-based: people enroll voice profiles, and the system reports confidence rather than pretending certainty. It is different from speech recognition, which identifies words rather than people.

Voiceprint / Voice Profile

A compact representation of a person’s voice used for matching later speech to an enrolled speaker. It should be treated as sensitive biometric-adjacent data.

Transcript Segment

A short chunk of transcript with timing metadata, such as start time, end time, speaker label, recognized text, and confidence.

JSONL

JSON Lines. A log format where each line is its own JSON object. It is useful for append-only event logs because new events can be written one at a time.

Event Log

An append-only record of what the system observed, inferred, decided, said, delayed, or suppressed. It makes the conversational machinery inspectable and replayable.

Surface Mind

The fast layer that stays close to the live conversation. It tracks speech activity, turn-taking, speaker state, and short social responses.

Deep Mind

The slower reasoning layer. It can think, retrieve memory, use tools, compare interpretations, and return candidate thoughts to the surface system.

Wonder Packet

A private internal question created by the surface mind, such as “Was that directed at me?” or “Is this the moment to mention the architecture analogy?”

Musing

A candidate thought produced by the deep mind. A musing is not automatically spoken; it waits for social evaluation.

Musing Queue

The working list of candidate thoughts waiting to be spoken, compressed, deferred, expired, or suppressed.

Social Governor

The layer that decides whether the system has standing to speak. It weighs urgency, importance, interruption risk, addressee, mode, and consent boundaries.

Turn-Taking

The conversational skill of knowing who has the floor, whether a pause is an opening, and whether speaking now would be helpful or interruptive.

Backchannel

A small response that shows attention without taking over the conversation, such as “mm-hm,” “right,” or “I’m with you.”

Paralinguistic Understanding

Understanding meaning carried by how something is said: timing, hesitation, laughter, stress, tone, pace, overlap, and emotional color.

Standing to Speak

The system’s estimate that it is socially appropriate to talk now. Direct address raises standing; overheard human-to-human conversation usually lowers it.

Recognition vs. Participation

Recognition identifies signals such as words, speakers, timestamps, or voices. Participation decides what to do with those signals in a live social scene: answer, wait, backchannel, remember, ask permission, or stay silent.
